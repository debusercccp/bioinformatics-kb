# Shaping diversity: gene flow

## 7.1 Colonization, migration and gene flow

**Colonization** is the movement into previously **unoccupied land** and leads to a **founder effect**. **Migration** is the movement into an **occupied land**; when migrants contribute to the next generation of the recipient population, the result is **gene flow**.

## 7.2 Effects of gene flow

- **Introducing alleles** into populations in which they were previously absent or **altering existing allele frequencies**.
- **Reducing genetic differentiation** among populations: gene flow can **counterbalance the effects of genetic drift**.
- Producing **very rapid changes** in allele frequencies: even a relatively low migration rate (*m*) can make the allele frequencies of two populations **nearly identical in few generations**.

With a symmetric migration rate (*m*) between two populations A and B, the allele frequencies in the next generation are:

*p<sub>A</sub>(t+1) = (1 − m) p<sub>A</sub>(t) + m p<sub>B</sub>(t)*

*p<sub>B</sub>(t+1) = m p<sub>A</sub>(t) + (1 − m) p<sub>B</sub>(t)*

## 7.3 Models of gene flow

![[SATAUMV-image15.png|288]] ![[SATAUMV-image16.png|254]]

| **Model** | **Key features** |
|:---|:---|
| n-island model | Discrete demes of size N exchange migrants at rate *m* with every other deme. |
| Stepping-stone model | Each deme exchanges migrants (*m*/4 in each direction) only with its immediate neighbours on a grid. |
| Isolation by distance | Migration within a continuous population: the probability that two individuals mate decreases with their distance. Genetic similarity develops in neighbourhoods as a function of dispersal distances, so close individuals tend to be more similar. When dispersal does not overcome the loss of alleles by drift, population differentiation results. |
