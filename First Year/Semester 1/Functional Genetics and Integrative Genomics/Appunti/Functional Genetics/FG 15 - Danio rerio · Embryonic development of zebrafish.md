# Embryonic development of zebrafish

The laboratory lecture follows the zebrafish embryo from fertilization to the larva, period by period, as it is seen under the stereomicroscope. The reference staging series is **Kimmel et al., 1995** (*Stages of embryonic development of the zebrafish*, Dev. Dyn. 203:253–310); the 3D illustrations of each period come from **Webb et al., 2007**. Two reasons why development is the strong point of the model:

- **rapid development**: the main organs are formed within **24 h**; sexual maturity in **3–4 months**;

- **evolutionarily conserved mechanisms**: an excellent model for **human pathology**.

![[FG-image51.png|464]]

*Stage series from zygote to 48 h (Kimmel et al., 1995).*

### The egg and the sequence of periods

- **Large yolky egg**, surrounded by the **vitelline membrane** and the **chorion**.

- **External fertilisation**. The sperm enters through the **micropyle** (*micropilum* on the slide), a channel in the chorion at the **animal pole**; the **cortical granule reaction** blocks polyspermy, and the chorion lifts away from the egg (egg diameter from **0.5 to 1 mm**).

- **Cytoplasmic reorganisation**: the non-yolky cytoplasm gathers at the animal pole to form the **blastodisc**.

The periods that follow: **zygote → cleavage → blastula → gastrula → segmentation (somitogenesis) → pharyngula (organogenesis) → hatching → larval stage**.

Time is counted in **hpf** (hours post fertilisation) and **dpf** (days post fertilisation): e.g. **48 hpf = 2 dpf**.

> **Focus — Why stages, not hours. Times refer to 28.5 °C; stage embryos by morphology, not by hpf.**

### Zygote period (0–¾ h)

- **Chorion swelling** after activation of the egg.

- **Cytoplasmic streaming** starts by about **10 min** after fertilisation: non-yolky cytoplasm moves towards the animal pole.

- The **blastodisc** forms at the animal pole. This defines **two compartments**: the **animal pole**, where the blastodisc forms, and the **vegetal pole** (the yolk).

> **Focus — The first axis is maternal. Both axes are set by maternal determinants before zygotic transcription.**

### Cleavage period (¾–2¼ h)

- **Meroblastic (discoidal) cleavage**: only the blastodisc divides, the yolk does not.

- The first division produces **two identical cells**, the **blastomeres**. Up to the **8-cell stage** the blastomeres remain **connected to the yolk** (the cleavage furrows are incomplete).

- The first **6 divisions are synchronous**, one every **15 min** (2 cells at 0.75 h, 4 at 1 h, 8 at 1.25 h, 16 at 1.5 h, 32 at 1.75 h, 64 at 2 h); after that cleavage becomes **metasynchronous**.

- The segmented blastodisc is now called the **blastoderm**. By the **32–64-cell stage** two distinct compartments are visible: the **deep cells** and the **enveloping layer (EVL)**, a superficial epithelial monolayer.

> **Focus — Why this matters for microinjection. 1-cell-stage injections spread to all cells; later single-blastomere injections give mosaics.**

### Blastula period (2¼–5¼ h)

- At the **512-cell stage** cells start to **divide asynchronously** and more slowly: this is the **midblastula transition (MBT)**.

- Formation of the **yolk syncytial layer (YSL)**: the marginal blastomeres collapse into the yolk cell and release their nuclei into a shared cytoplasm at the yolk surface. The YSL is extraembryonic but signals to the overlying cells.

- The YSL moves up, the yolk bulges towards the animal pole (**dome** stage) and **epiboly starts**: the blastoderm thins and spreads over the yolk towards the vegetal pole.

At this point the embryo has **three cell populations**: the **EVL** (outer protective layer, extraembryonic), the **deep cells** (which form the embryo proper) and the **YSL**.

> **Focus — MBT: maternal control hands over to zygotic control. The zygotic genome switches on at the MBT; maternal products can mask phenotypes (hence MZ mutants).**

### Gastrula period (5¼–10 h)

- **Epiboly** goes on (50% epiboly at about 5¼ h) until it reaches **100%**, when the blastoderm covers the whole yolk.

- **Involution**: at the margin the deep cells fold under, forming the **germ ring**.

- **Convergence** begins: cells move towards the future dorsal side and thicken into the **embryonic shield**, the zebrafish counterpart of the **dorsal lip of the blastopore** in amphibians (the **organiser**).

- **Two layers** are formed:

  - **epiblast** (outer) → **ectoderm**;

  - **hypoblast** (inner, *ipoblast* on the slide) → **mesoderm** and **endoderm**.

- **Convergence and extension**: the axial hypoblast elongates along the anterior–posterior axis. Gastrulation ends with the **tail bud** stage (**bud**, 10 h).

> **Focus — Shield = organiser. A transplanted shield induces a secondary axis.**

### Segmentation period / somitogenesis (10–24 h)

Organogenesis begins.

- **Somitogenesis**: about **1 somite every half hour**, from anterior to posterior. In this period embryos are staged by counting somites.

- **Neurulation**: **neural plate → neural keel → neural rod → neural tube**.

- **Neuromeres**: **telencephalon**, **diencephalon**, **mesencephalon** (midbrain) + **7 posterior neuromeres**, the **rhombomeres** of the **rhombencephalon** (hindbrain).

> **Focus — Two things that differ from the textbook frog. The neural tube forms by cavitation, not folding; somites come from a segmentation clock (her1/her7, ~30 min).**

### Pharyngula period (24–48 h)

![[FG-image52.png|441]]

*Prim-6 (25 h) and long-pec (48 h) embryos.*

- **Phylotypic stage**: the **typical vertebrate body plan**, when embryos of different vertebrates look most alike.

- **Bilaterality**, **notochord**, **5 encephalic vesicles**, **pharyngeal/branchial arches**.

- **Pigmentation** begins.

- **Sensory placodes of the head**: **olfactory**, **optic** and **otic** placode.

- **Lateral line**: a series of **neuromasts** (mechanosensory organs) set in channels along the body; it derives from a **placode that migrates from the otic region to the tail**, depositing neuromasts as it goes.

- **Fin fold** (median) and **fin buds** (pectoral).

- **Cardiocirculatory system**: the heart beats and blood circulates.

- **Behavioural development**: the embryo is sensitive to touch (prim-6 stage, about 25 h). By **48 h** (long-pec stage) the pectoral fin buds are visible.

- **Hatching**: the slide lists *24–48 h*, but in Kimmel’s series and in the figure on the same slide (Gilbert) the embryo **hatches between 48 and 72 h**, so check this point with the lecturer.

> **Focus — Keeping the embryo transparent. PTU or pigment mutants (nacre, casper).**
>
> **When the larva becomes a protected animal.** After hatching the larva lives on its yolk; at about **5 dpf** it starts **feeding independently**. Under EU Directive 2010/63/EU this is the point from which zebrafish larvae count as protected animals, so experiments on embryos before this stage are not regulated as animal experiments. This is one of the reasons why so many screens are done in the first 5 days.

### Summary of the periods

| Period | Time (hpf, 28.5 °C) | Key events |
|----|----|----|
| Zygote | 0–¾ | Chorion swelling, cytoplasmic streaming, blastodisc at the animal pole |
| Cleavage | ¾–2¼ | Meroblastic cleavage; 6 synchronous divisions (15 min); deep cells and EVL |
| Blastula | 2¼–5¼ | Asynchronous divisions from 512 cells (MBT); YSL; dome, epiboly starts |
| Gastrula | 5¼–10 | Involution, germ ring, shield (organiser); epiblast/hypoblast; convergence-extension; bud stage |
| Segmentation | 10–24 | Somites (1 every 30 min); neurulation (keel, rod, tube); neuromeres |
| Pharyngula | 24–48 | Phylotypic stage; pigmentation; placodes; lateral line; heartbeat; fin buds |
| Hatching | 48–72 | Exit from the chorion |
| Larva | from 72 | Free swimming; independent feeding from about 5 dpf |
