# Introduction to Caenorhabditis elegans

**General description.** *"You have made your way from worm to man, and much in you is still worm"* (Friedrich Nietzsche, 1844–1900). *Caenorhabditis elegans* is a nematode: a smooth-skinned worm with a long, unsegmented, cylindrical body tapered at both ends. Comprising about 1,000 cells, it is the most primitive animal to exhibit characteristics that are important in the study of human biology and disease. Though tiny and transparent, *C. elegans* contains a full set of differentiated tissues, including a nervous system with a "brain," which allows the study of behavior in a worm that is capable of learning. It is found worldwide in soil and rotting vegetation.

**Nobel Moment (2002).** The 2002 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded for key discoveries concerning the genetic regulation of organ development and programmed cell death in *C. elegans*:

- **Sydney Brenner** established the nematode as a novel experimental model organism.

- **John Sulston** mapped a cell lineage in which every cell division and differentiation could be followed.

- **Robert Horvitz** discovered and characterized key genes controlling cell death.

**Feature technology – RNAi.** *C. elegans* is ideal for the study of functional genomics: the genome sequence is complete and well-annotated, and systematic gene function can be studied by RNA interference-mediated knockdown. RNA interference (RNAi) is a gene-silencing technique that uses double-stranded RNA to degrade the corresponding messenger RNA, leading to protein depletion and a loss-of-function phenotype.

### Who is *Caenorhabditis elegans*? Biology and Laboratory Cultivation

**Identity and general traits:**

- **Etymology:** *Caeno* = recent; *rhabditis* = stick; *elegans* = elegant.

- **Ecology:** Non-parasitic, soil-dwelling nematode found in temperate climates.

- **Standard lab strain:** Common lab strain (**N2**) was isolated in the UK from a compost heap.

- **Physical and culture traits:** Small (1 mm) and transparent.

- **Nutrition and environment:** Eats bacteria, can be grown easily on agar plates or in liquid media, and requires controlled humidity, temperature, and oxygen.

### Why is *C. elegans* a good model organism?

1.  **Compact size**.

2.  **Short life cycle**.

3.  **Large brood size**.

4.  **Easy handling** (manually transferred using a platinum wire pick sterilized over a Bunsen burner).

5.  **Low cost**.

6.  **No required approvals** from an Institutional Animal Care & Use Committee (IACUC).

**Crucial experimental consideration.** The most important factor to consider when working with *C. elegans* as a model organism is **sterility and controlled experimental conditions**.

**Cultivation in the laboratory:**

- *C. elegans* can be grown quickly in laboratories because it has minimal nutritional requirements.

- It is maintained on **Nematode Growth Medium (NGM)** agar plated into Petri dishes of different diameters and can also grow in liquid medium.

- Agar plates are seeded with ***E. coli* OP50** as food, with different amounts of bacterial solution plated based on the plate diameter.

### Anatomy, Sex Determination, and Sexual Dimorphism

**Adult body structure:**

- Consists of **two concentric tubes** separated by a **pseudocoelomic cavity**.

- **Inner tube:** digestive system (pharynx and gut).

- **Outer tube:** cuticle, hypodermis, muscle wall, and nervous system.

- **Pseudocoelomic cavity:** contains the gonads, eggs, and vulva.

- **Important Note:** *C. elegans* has **no circulatory or respiratory system** and have an **unsegmented body**.

**Chromosomes and sex determination:**

- *C. elegans* has **6 pairs of chromosomes** (autosomes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and sex chromosome X).

- **Important Note:** Sex is determined by the **ratio of autosomes to X chromosomes**.

- **XX** $\Rightarrow$ **Hermaphrodites:** capable of self-fertilization (in diakinesis, 6 bivalents are present in both oogenesis and spermatogenesis).

- **X0** $\Rightarrow$ **Males:** originate from the spontaneous loss of an X chromosome (in diakinesis, 5 bivalents and 1 univalent X chromosome are present in spermatogenesis).

- **Selfing vs crossing:** Self-fertilization of hermaphrodites always produces hermaphrodites, whereas crossing a hermaphrodite with a male results in a progeny with **50% males and 50% hermaphrodites**.

**Sexual dimorphism in *C. elegans*:**

- **Somatic cell counts:** Hermaphrodite has **959 somatic cells**; Male has **1,031 somatic cells**.

- **Tail morphology:** Hermaphrodite has a **pointed tail**; Male worm has a **rounded tail**.

- **Hermaphrodite reproductive system:** acts as a modified female: it produces **sperm at the L4 stage** (which are stored in the **spermathecae**), then switches to **oocyte production** in adulthood, fertilizing oocytes with the stored sperm. Its anatomy includes two symmetric gonad arms capped by **Distal Tip Cells**, oocytes, spermathecae, sperm, and a mid-body **vulva**.

- **Male reproductive system:** Single-armed gonad with **Distal Tip Cells**, **testis**, **spermatocytes**, **seminal vesicle**, and **vas deferens** opening at the copulatory tail.

### Biological Life Cycle and Larval Stages

**Microscopy and temperature-dependent growth.** *C. elegans* can be visualized with a **stereomicroscope** for general morphology and behavior, while **fluorescence microscopy** (e.g., confocal or wide-field fluorescence) enables the visualization of specific cellular and molecular processes using fluorescent proteins like GFP. *C. elegans* grows between **16°C and 25°C**, and the growth rate increases with temperature (~5.5 days at 15°C, ~3.5 days at 20°C, and ~2.5 days at 25°C).

**Responses to nutritional deprivation and stress:**

- **L1 arrest:** If no food is present at hatching, L1 larvae enter **L1 arrest**.

- **Dauer stage:** Induced at the L1/L2 molt (via a 13 hr **Predauer L2d** intermediate) under conditions of **overcrowding, starvation (nutritional deprivation), or high temperature**. The dauer larva (~400 µm) is **darker, thinner, and immobile**, and features a thicker, **impermeable cuticle**, sealed orifices, gonad development arrest, cessation of germline stem cell (GSC) proliferation, high stress resistance, and extended survival (**3 to 4 months**). Upon **refeeding**, the dauer larva exits arrest and molts directly into the **L4 stage**.

- **L4 starvation:** Under starvation at the L4 stage (crowding not required), worms progress to adulthood, GSC proliferation arrests, and the gonad shrinks progressively.

### Interesting Concepts: Cell Lineage and Programmed Cell Death (Apoptosis)

**1. Cell Lineage and Differentiation**

The origin and fate of every single cell in *C. elegans* are known. The complete **cell lineage map** was determined by **John E. Sulston** (1983), who shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

- **Founder cells:** Divisions of the zygote ($P_{0}$) generate the founder blastomeres **AB** (mostly pharyngeal cells, neurons or glial cells, and hypodermal/epidermal cells), **MS** (mesodermal cells and the post-embryonic **M** lineage that forms the gonad and muscles), **E** (endodermal cells), **C**, **D**, and $P_{3}/P_{4}$ (germline).

**2. Programmed Cell Death (Apoptosis)**

- Apoptosis, or programmed cell death, is an essential component of *C. elegans* development.

- It occurs during two phases of *C. elegans* life and in two different tissue types: (1) during embryonic and post-embryonic development of the soma (**"developmental cell death"**), and (2) in the gonads of adult hermaphrodites (**"germ cell death"**).

- Cells undergoing apoptosis change their morphology and refractive properties, making them easily observable in living worms with a phase-contrast (**Nomarski**) microscope.

**Embryonic cell lineage and apoptosis counts (from zygote** $P_{0}$ **to hatching):**

- **AB (**$\text{ABa} + \text{ABp}$**):** 72 hypodermal, 254 neuronal, 23 muscle, 40 structural/other, **98 deaths** (389 total surviving).

- **E:** 20 intestine (**0 deaths**).

- **MS:** 48 muscle, 13 neuronal, 9 gland, 2 somatic gonad, 8 other, **14 deaths** (80 total surviving).

- **C:** 32 muscle, 13 hypodermal, 2 neuronal, **1 death** (47 total surviving).

- **D:** 20 muscle (**0 deaths**).

- $P_{4}$**:** 2 germ line (**0 deaths**).

- **Total embryonic counts:** **671 total cells made** $\rightarrow$ **113 programmed cell deaths (17%)** $\rightarrow$ **558 cells at hatching**.

**The genetic pathway for programmed cell death in *C. elegans* (Ellis et al., 1991; Hengartner & Horvitz, 1994):**

- **Decision to die (individual cell types):** Regulated by cell-specific genes such as $\text{ces-2} \dashv \text{ces-1} \rightarrow \text{ced-9}$ in NSM sister cells and $\text{egl-1} \dashv \text{ced-9}$ in hermaphrodite HSN neurons.

- **Execution of death (all dying cells):** $\text{ced-9} \dashv \text{ced-4},\text{ced-3}$ (along with $\text{ced-8}$) $\rightarrow$ dead cell.

- **Engulfment (by engulfing cell):** Mediated by two parallel gene groups: ***ced-1, ced-6, ced-7*** and ***ced-2, ced-5, ced-10***.

- **Degradation:** DNA and cellular remnants are degraded in the engulfing cell by ***nuc-1***.

**Evolutionary conservation (Important Note – CED-3, CED-4, and CED-9 have human counterparts):**

| **Role** | **C. elegans** | **Vertebrates / Humans** | **Function** |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Regulator** | **CED-9** | **Bcl-2** | Anti-apoptotic protein that inhibits CED-4/Apaf-1 |
| **Adapter** | **CED-4** | **Apaf-1** (Apoptotic protease activating factor 1) | Adapter protein that activates the executioner protease |
| **Effector** | **CED-3** | **Caspases (Casp9** $\rightarrow$ **Casp3)** | Executioner proteases that cleave substrates to cause cell death |

**Summary of *C. elegans* advantages:** Simple anatomy, transparent body, short life cycle, small size, and easy to manipulate (2002 Nobel Prize winners: Sydney Brenner, John Sulston, and Bob Horvitz for pioneering research in genetics, neurobiology, and apoptosis).

### Transferring worms on NGM plates:

- **1) Picking:** lifting individual worms under a stereomicroscope using a platinum wire pick or a mounted **eyelash**.

- **2) Chunking:** cutting out a piece of agar with a sterile scalpel/forceps and transferring it to a fresh NGM plate.
